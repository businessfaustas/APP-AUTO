MATERIALS

Cast.

What cast film is

Cast film is made by pouring liquid vinyl onto a casting sheet and letting it set, so it carries no built-in tension. Calendered film is rolled and stretched out of a thicker mass, and it keeps trying to shrink back. On a flat door the difference takes months to show. In a recess or round a mirror, it shows in weeks.

Why 70 microns

70 µm is the balance point. Thin enough to stretch into curves and lie flat in panel gaps without bridging. Thick enough to stay stable while it's positioned and to hide fine texture on the surface underneath.

Why recesses are post-heated

Stretched film remembers its flat shape. Heating the stretched areas after application, to the film maker's post-heat temperature, erases that memory. Without it, film in a recess lifts weeks later, which is usually when a cheap wrap starts to show.

HANDOVER

Every car leaves
with a passport

A wrap that isn't documented can't be matched or repaired properly. Film colours shift slightly between production batches, so a damaged panel redone from a different roll can sit a shade off its neighbours.

The passport records the exact film code and batch, the date it was applied, and the care protocol for that film. It's for you, for the next installer, and for whoever buys the car after you.
