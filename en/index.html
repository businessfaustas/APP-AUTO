MATERIALS

Cast.
70 µm.
Post-heated.

What cast film is

Cast film is made by pouring liquid vinyl onto a casting sheet and letting it set, so it carries no built-in tension. Calendered film is rolled and stretched out of a thicker mass, and it keeps trying to shrink back. On a flat door the difference takes months to show. In a recess or round a mirror, it shows in weeks.

Why 70 microns

70 µm is the balance point. Thin enough to stretch into curves and lie flat in panel gaps without bridging. Thick enough to stay stable while it's positioned and to hide fine texture on the surface underneath.

Why recesses are post-heated

Stretched film remembers its flat shape. Heating the stretched areas after application, to the film maker's post-heat temperature, erases that memory. Without it, film in a recess lifts weeks later, which is usually when a cheap wrap starts to show.

PROCESS — 05 STEPS

How the work runs

  1. 01

    Inspection and a written price

    We look at the car or your photos, agree the film and the colour, and list every part that comes off. The price comes in writing before work starts.

  2. 02

    Design on the real panels

    For livery and graphics, the design is drawn on your car's measured panels, not a stock side view. You approve the proof before anything is printed or cut.

  3. 03

    Disassembly and preparation

    Handles, lights, badges and trim come off. Panels are cleaned and degreased so the adhesive bonds to paint, not to dirt or wax.

  4. 04

    Application and post-heating

    Cast film goes on, wraps round each panel edge and is post-heated in every recess and stretched area, so it stays where it was laid.

  5. 05

    Handover with the material passport

    Parts go back on. The car leaves with its material passport, so the next person who touches the film knows exactly what it is.
